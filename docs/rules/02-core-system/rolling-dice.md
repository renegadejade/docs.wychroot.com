# Rolling Dice

Whenever there is uncertainty in the fiction, you roll dice to find out what happens. Rolls are made with pools of six-sided dice (d6). Rolls are notated with a number followed by the letter ‘d,’ so ‘3d’ means you roll three dice. In most cases, the number of dice you roll will be determined by a trait _rating_ and may be modified further by certain rules.
When a rule tells you to take **+1**, add a die to the pool before you roll. Similarly, if told to take **-1d**, remove a die from the pool before rolling.
Once your dice pool is assembled, roll and take the _single highest result_, comparing it to the results table for the type of roll you're making. While there a many types of roll, most follow the same basic pattern:

  Occasionally, your dice pool will have zero dice, or “0d”. When this happens, roll 2d and judge the lowest result.

:::info A note on crits

You can only get a **CRIT** when you have _edge_ on a roll.

:::

### Edge

Certain moves, gear, and situations can give a roll _edge_, which is notated as “+E”. When a roll has edge, you increase the result of the roll by 1 before comparing it to the results table. A result of 1 becomes a 2, a result of 2 becomes 3, and so on.
When the result is a 6, and you have edge, increase it to 7 as normal. This is called a critical hit, or **crit**. When you crit on a roll, you not only get what you want, but you also get something extra, like great impact, more information, or other bonus.

> Having _edge_ on a roll that upgrades the result from a 6 to a 7 is the only way get a _crit_.

### Player Rolls

- **Action rolls** are when you do something risky that could have consequences. You combine an essence with a skill and roll to find out what happens. Action rolls have a risk level that determines the severity of consequences if you a glitch or an error. (pXX)
  -crafting rolls **Crafting rolls** are made during downtime when you choose your crafting moves for the phase. Each crafting roll has an associated skill and you roll it to see much progress you make on your crafting projects. (pXX)

### GM Rolls

- **Entanglement rolls** are made at the beginning of the direct action phase to see how the PCs previous exploits will cause complications for them during the action
- **Disengagement rolls** are made when the direct action is over and its time to head home, to see how the crew fares while extracting themselves to safety
  ||- **Wyrd rolls** are made to answer questions about the world when the GM isn’t sure or wants to leave it up to the winds of fate, or _wyrd_||

To determine the results of a roll, take the single highest die value and compare it to the results table for that type of roll to see how things went. If you roll two or more 6s, the result is a critical. If the highest roll is a 6, the result is a success. If the highest roll is a 4 or a 5, the result is mixed. If the the highest roll is a 1, 2, or 3, the result is a failure.
Critical — Yes, and…
Success — Yes.
Mixed — Yes, but…
Failure — No, and…
In some cases, such as when you have zero dots in an action rating, you roll “0d”. In this case, roll two six-sided dice and take the lower value when determining the result. Additionally, when you roll 0d, the result cannot be a critical.
When judging the result of a roll, the result determines the outcome for the entity that the player or GM rolled for. In most cases, this means a player rolling for their character — a critical is the best possible outcome for the character, whereas failure is the worst possible outcome for the character. GM rolls, however, cover several cases — sometimes rolling for NPC actions, factions moves, and outcomes that are working with the PCs, and, sometimes, against the PCs. For example, when the GM rolls to see the outcome of a faction roll, read the the result from the point of view of the faction: a critical means things went well for the faction and a failure means things went poorly for the faction.
